# Changelog

## Unreleased

- feat: deformable-dirt sculpting (prototype). The sand floor is now a subdivided heightfield you can shape by hand with three new toy-box brushes: Pile pushes sand up (drag along the outside of a turn to raise a berm), Carve scoops it away, and Smooth eases it back toward flat - each a soft radial brush you drag over the sand. Sculpting is undoable (each stroke is one Undo) and Reset flattens the terrain. Directional relief shading is baked into the surface so piles and hollows read from the top-down camera. This is the foundation the removed Berm stamp pointed at (issue #29/#31); jumps and other stamped features still sit on top as separate objects for now, and the relief read is intentionally soft - stronger sculpt shaping and shading are the next iteration.
- change: remove the stamped Berm tool from both prototypes. A berm is sculpted dirt, not a preset, so it should be made by hand (pile dirt on the outside of a turn with the shovel/rake/dozer, then smooth the face) - part of the terrain-sculpting workflow (issue #29/#31). Berm removed from the web toy box (tool, icon, geometry, difficulty, feedback) and the Godot build (tool panel, placement, Obstacle rendering/difficulty/labels, track-name suggestions, rider feedback). The Godot save loader still tolerates any old "berm" data - it degrades to a generic bump. `docs/building_tools.md` and the roadmap record the decision.
- feat: softer, redesigned track look. The track was a heavy dark reddish-brown ribbon (near-black ruts, a hard dark border, a dark-red surface) that fought the warm sand. It is now a soft groomed sandy path: a warm shoulder that blends into the floor instead of a hard edge, a light packed-sand surface that reuses the sand's raked grooming, and two gentle wheel ruts instead of dark gouges. The sculpted dirt features (jumps, tabletop, berm, hill, rhythm sections) were warmed and lightened to match, so the whole track reads as one cohesive, cozy warm-dirt palette.
- feat: photo mode. A camera button (or the P key) turns the sandbox into a keepsake: the toolbox and HUD fade away, the diorama is framed like a cream Polaroid with the track name and a kid-style caption ("best track ever"), and Save photo composites a warm golden-hour shot - vignette, warm wash, the track name and caption baked in - and downloads it as a PNG. Works mid-race for the "the sandbox came alive" money shot. Esc or Done exits. First piece of the Version 5 photo-mode roadmap; matches `docs/SCREENSHOT_PHILOSOPHY.md`.
- feat: roost spray + per-brand bike colourways. Racing bikes now throw a fan of light sand out onto the ground beside the track (roost), thrown to the outside in corners and on landings - it reads against the dark dirt where the old ridden-line marks couldn't. And each bike is now a distinct fictional toy brand (Toy Bike Bible: Legacy Moto, TrailWorks, DirtCo, PitKid Racing, MotoForge, Backyard Factory): a primary colour plus a contrasting accent applied as rim tape, a shroud stripe, a tank stripe, and a helmet stripe, so the pack reads as a shelf of different collectible bikes. The kid-simple colour name ("the Red bike") still drives feedback.
- feat: tactile sand + warmer light. Building now combs the sand: drawing the track leaves light raked grooves trailing alongside the stroke, and placing or dozing a toy leaves a combed ring where the sand was pushed around it (issue #33). Races wear the dirt in further - bikes leave darker scuffs where they come down from a jump, on top of the existing ridden-line marks. And the whole diorama got a warmth pass toward the Sandcastle golden-hour look: warmer sky/earth ambient, a more golden key light with softer shadows, a warmer fill, and slightly higher exposure.
- feat: premium toy-dirtbike + rider overhaul. The racers were a stack of boxes and capsules; they're now collectible 1:12 toy motocross bikes with a real dirtbike silhouette (grounded in actual MX proportions): two big equal knobby wheels on a long wheelbase with an open frame triangle you can see through, raked telescopic forks that push the front wheel forward, a small narrow tank, a long thin flat seat, angled radiator shrouds (the prominent side color, not a slab), a kicked-up rear tail, a small floating front fender, a compact central engine with a slanted head, a chrome exhaust header sweeping to a rear muffler, and real plastic number plates (front + both sides) carrying each rider's racing number from a per-bike canvas texture. The rider sits on the bike leaning into the bars: matching jersey, chunky boots on the pegs, gloves on the grips, oversized helmet with a color stripe and dark visor. Racing numbers narrowed to 1-99 for readable plates; all toy-animation behavior (bounce, wheelie, air tilt, landing squash, roost) is unchanged.
- feat: toy-box menu icons + object overhaul. The toy-box buttons now show little illustrations of what they build - a wavy track, a red start gate with a go-light, a checkered finish flag, dirt kicker ramps (single/double/triple), a flat-topped tabletop, washboard whoops, rounded rollers, a rippled sand pile, a banked berm, a rounded hill, a bulldozer, and a back-arrow undo - drawn as inline SVGs in the warm sandbox palette instead of ASCII symbols. In the 3D sandbox, start and finish are now proper toy gates (rounded capped posts with a red "go" banner / a checkered finish banner) instead of a checker box row; whoops and rollers are sculpted swept rhythm sections instead of half-sphere lumps; and the world props got a premium-toy pass: a real bucket with a rim, arched handle, and sand inside; a rounder dump truck with a tilted bed and sand load; a rake leaning in the sand (the tool that combs the grooves); a capped plastic shovel; and grassy tufts along the rim.
- feat: sculpted dirt features + tactile sand (graphics pass toward the Sandcastle bar). Jumps no longer look like blobby mounds: singles are kickers with a takeoff face, a rounded lip, and a landing slope; doubles/triples are takeoff-plus-landing sequences; the tabletop is a flat-topped ramp with a sun-lit worn line; the berm is a banked curved wall instead of a ring of lumps; the hill is a smooth rounded rise. Web build (`web/game.js`) sweeps real side profiles into soft-bevelled dirt geometry with warm packed-dirt material; the sandbox floor gains raked/combed grooves (issue #33) and a warmer golden tone. Godot 2D build (`scripts/Obstacle.gd`) gets matching shaded ramp silhouettes, a banked-berm read, soft ground shadows, and the warmer palette. New `docs/REFERENCES.md` documents Sandcastle (Bubblebird Studio) as the primary touchstone and the graphics bar; `docs/visual_style_guide.md` already named it as the target.
- feat: living camera. The sandbox now breathes when the player's hands are still - a slow, imperfect drift, breathing zoom, gentle sway, and faint tilt, like kneeling beside a diorama and admiring it. It hushes the instant the player touches the sand (draw, place, pan, zoom, or hover) so building stays crisp and direct, and eases back in after a short beat of stillness. During a race the camera softly follows the pack and leans back for readability, then returns the framing to the player. Answers the "the game should act like this" reference clip. New `docs/BEHAVIOR_SPEC.md` captures the behavior contract and acceptance criteria; `docs/CAMERA_DESIGN.md` gains an Idle Life section.

- feat: kid-style track names. Every sandbox now has a name on a cozy nameplate: suggested from what is actually on the track (a triple can roll "The Impossible Triple", sand rolls "Big Sand SX"), editable by clicking and typing (28-char cap, camera keys pause while typing), rerollable with New Name, persisted in the save file's reserved `track_name` field, and used to caption photo mode Polaroids. First Memory Book piece. Headless test in `tests/track_name_smoke.gd`.
- feat: toy rider animation. Bikes now animate like toys brought to life, straight from the toy bike bible: slight bounce while riding (wobblier for low-consistency riders), takeoff wheelies, whole-bike tilt in the air (nose up then nose down), happy landing squash bounces with sand puffs, roost spray behind the rear wheel, crash dust clouds, and a soft shadow under airborne bikes. Hand-drawn capped particles, no physics. Headless test in `tests/rider_animation_smoke.gd`.
- feat: obstacle move and pick-up tools. The kid's hand can now reach in: Hand / Move drags a placed toy to a new spot, Pick Up lifts it out of the sandbox, both leave a little dent in the sand, show a hover ring, support Undo, and autosave/persist like any other build change. Tool panel compacted so all tools fit a 720p window. Headless test in `tests/obstacle_edit_smoke.gd`.
